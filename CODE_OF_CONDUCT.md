# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person on the other end is trying to do
something reasonable and does not have the context you have.

## What is expected

- Treat disagreement as being about the work, not the person.
- Accept that maintainers get to say no, and that "no" is often about scope
  rather than merit.
- Give feedback on code, not on the person who wrote it.
- Respect that contributors here are volunteers working across time zones and
  other obligations.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, personal attacks, or demeaning comments, public or private.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or review.
- Posting other people's prompts, transcripts, or session data without their
  consent — this project reads transcripts, so treat that data as personal.

## Scope

This applies in the repository's issues, pull requests, discussions, and
commits, and anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately to the maintainer via a GitHub private advisory on
the [Security tab](https://github.com/schmug/contextbuddy/security) or by
direct message. Reports are handled discreetly. Responses range from a private
word to a permanent ban from the repository, depending on severity and pattern.

The maintainer is the final arbiter here, and will recuse from deciding
reports about their own conduct.

---

For a fuller, more formal alternative, the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/) is the standard
text; GitHub can add it to this repository with its built-in code of conduct
template.
